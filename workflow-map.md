# Current workflow map: Routine policyholder correspondence

**Status: Reviewed** (approved by the user, 2026-10-06). v2, with the exclusion of automatic sending added. Sources: [workflow-description.md](workflow-description.md) (you confirmed it as right on 2026-10-06), the course workflow diagram ([case-context.md](case-context.md)), your answers, and the Reviewed plans. This maps **current practice, without the AI draft**. No redesign and no new AI uses.

## Agreed scope
- **Included:** routine letters to policyholders after a claim decision is approved.
- **Excluded:** conflict and legal cases; replies to policyholder concerns and end-of-day updates (your decision, 2026-10-05); policy lookup support (Next); sending letters automatically without human approval (your addition, 2026-10-06; your message ended at "Without human", so I've read it as without handler review and approval).
- **Workflow owner:** Claims Correspondence Operations Manager (course role; **not yet confirmed by them**).
- **People affected:** claims handlers (40 in the pilot) and policyholders; team leaders, managers and compliance reviewers (roles unresolved, Q4).
- **Start:** a claim decision is approved. **End:** the letter is sent.

## Map

| Step and person responsible | What happens | Problems or decisions |
|---|---|---|
| **1. Gather information.** Claims handler | The handler collects the approved decision, policy and customer details, case notes and required wording from authorised systems. **Sources (your account):** user profile database, past claims, past premiums, and claim-note details checked against policies; also company policies and the handlers' own learnings. **Handoff in:** the approved claim decision. | **Problem:** repeated switching and copying between systems (course). The systems involved are several, so this is the evidenced cause of the switching. Handlers also struggle to find the right policyholder and policy details across databases (you). **Decision:** none recorded. |
| **2. Draft the letter.** Claims handler | The handler selects or recreates suitable wording. **Inputs:** the details gathered in step 1 and wording from earlier cases. | **Problem:** similar wording is recreated across cases (course). Your account is that drafting (syntax and tone) takes most of the time. Sampled median drafting time before the pilot was 18 minutes. **Decision:** the handler chooses the wording. |
| **3. Review the draft.** Claims handler | The handler checks facts, tone and required statements against the same sources. | **Problem:** details can be hard to trace to their sources (course). **Decision (human judgement):** whether the facts, tone and required statements are right. |
| **4. Approve or refer.** Claims handler | The handler approves the routine letter, or refers an unusual case for specialist review. **Handoff out:** a referral goes to a specialist (who receives it is unknown). An approved letter is sent. | **Problem:** referral processes vary by region (course). **Decisions (human approval):** the handler decides whether the case is routine and approves every letter. The legal specialist decides legal conflict cases, which are out of scope. |

**Across the flow**
- **Elapsed time:** the median from approval to send is 2.4 business days. **Where those days go is not measured.** I haven't attributed the delay to any step. Drafting alone (18 minutes sampled) can't account for it.

## Confirmed facts (you or the course, or Reviewed)

- The handler checks and approves every letter. Conflict and legal cases are out of scope.

## Assumptions and missing information to confirm with the workflow owner
**Assumptions**
1. The four steps match practice in every region.
2. "Own learnings" means handlers' experience, not a system.
3. A single handler does all four steps for a letter. The diagram says "the handler" but doesn't say it is the same person throughout.

**Missing**
1. Where the elapsed days go between approval and send (waiting, queues, rework or something else).
2. Referral rules: what makes a case unusual, who receives it, how regions differ.
3. Where compliance reviewers and team leaders fit (Q4).
4. The exact systems behind the sources, and whether the letter is sent in the same action as approval or in a separate step with a wait.
5. Whether the workflow owner agrees they own the workflow's results.
